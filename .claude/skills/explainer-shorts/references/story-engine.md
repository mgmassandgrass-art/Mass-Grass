# The story engine

A sequence of pretty scenes is a slideshow. This is what makes it a video people finish.

Every number here comes from real transcripts and view data of high-performing explainer shorts, not from theory.

## The operating spec

| Variable | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hook window | 0-3 seconds. The stay-or-scroll decision happens here. |
| Hook length | 10-14 words, spoken in under 3 seconds |
| Narration pace | **170-200 WPM**, faster than documentary standard, because this competes with an infinite scroll |
| Word budget | **~3 words per second** of finished video |
| Visual change | Something changes every **2-4 seconds**. Past 4 seconds static is the drop-off danger zone |
| Target length | **45-60 seconds** is the genuine cross-platform sweet spot |
| Reading level | Grade 5-8 |
| Sentence length | Under ~14 words average |
| Ideas per video | Exactly one. Never two takeaways |

## Topic selection

A topic works when it has a **concrete anchor** (a real object, place, or number you can put on screen in the first 3 seconds) plus an **implied hidden mechanism**. Curiosity is not triggered by not knowing something; it is triggered by becoming aware of a specific gap you could close.

### The five categories that reliably work

Ranked by engagement rate, which matters more than raw views for a repeatable system.

1. **Money and economics of familiar things** (6-7% engagement). Rent, groceries, a paycheck, a commodity. Formula: *familiar thing + the hidden number nobody explained to you.*
2. **Hidden systems** (4-6%). Something everyone assumes just exists, revealed as designed by specific people for specific reasons. Borders, supply chains, standards.
3. **"Why does X look/work like Y"** (4-6%). An everyday object with a counterintuitive engineering or history reason. Formula: *you've seen this a thousand times, here's the detail you never noticed and why it's there.*
4. **Geopolitics and places you've never thought about** (2-4%, but the highest raw reach). Formula: *obscure place + why a superpower suddenly cares.*
5. **Historical surprises and object backstories** (2-4%). Formula: *[everyday object] took [surprising amount of accidents/people/centuries] to become the thing in your hand.*

Mechanism and place topics get the biggest raw reach because they are universally curious and non-polarizing. Money and hidden-system topics get 2-3x the engagement because viewers see themselves in them.

### What kills a topic

- **No concrete anchor.** If you cannot point at a specific object, place, or number in the first 3 seconds, it is too abstract. Convert the concept to its consequence: not "the semiconductor industry" but "why cars got more expensive."
- **Two ideas fighting.** The single most common explainer failure. Pick the sharper one.
- **Jargon-first framing.** If a term needs explaining before the hook lands, the hook is already lost.
- **Requiring prior knowledge.** Never underestimate the audience's intelligence; never overestimate their prior knowledge.
- **No emotional payoff class.** If it cannot deliver "whoa" or "that explains my life," it is a volume play, not a virality play.

## The beat map

```
HOOK        0:00-0:03    Visual anchor + tension. No throat-clearing.
CONTEXT     0:03-0:08    One sentence of orientation. What, where, who.
ESCALATION  0:08-~75%    2-4 beats. One fact each. Each raises stakes or adds a "but".
PAYOFF      ~75-90%      The reveal the hook promised. Biggest number or image in the video.
BUTTON      final 3-5s   Land on it and stop. NO summary.
```

### Word budget per beat

| Length | Total words | Hook | Context | Escalation | Payoff | Button |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30s | 85-100 | 8-10 | 12-15 | 30-40 (1-2 beats) | 20-25 | 8-10 |
| 45s | 127-150 | 10-12 | 15-18 | 55-70 (2-3 beats) | 30-35 | 8-10 |
| 60s | 170-200 | 10-14 | 18-22 | 80-100 (3-4 beats) | 40-45 | 10-12 |

### The open loop

The hook states a concrete thing plus an unresolved how or why. Every escalation beat withholds the mechanism one notch longer. The payoff **returns to the literal object from the hook**, so the ending reads as resolution rather than a new topic.

Land a small concrete proof by second 8-10 even while the big payoff waits for the end. A video whose first real value arrives too late loses people in the body, not the hook.

## Hook formulas

Pick one. Write three variants. Keep the one with the most concrete noun in it.

**1. Demonstrative anchor.** `"This is [concrete visible thing]."`
> "This is a modern nuclear weapon."
> "This is what a $25 million a year salary looks like versus a $25,000 a year salary."

**2. Named-entity intrigue.** `"This [thing] is called [evocative name]. It used to be [deflating fact]."`
> "This little choke point is called the Bab-el-Mandeb, or Gate of Tears. It used to be nothing but a recipe for shipwrecks."

**3. Reversal.** `"If you thought [assumption], think again."`

**4. Direct-address mock pitch.** `"Are you [role] trying to [goal]? [Unexpected answer]."`
> "Are you a global superpower looking to project influence in the newly opening Arctic? Look no further than Greenland."

**5. Cost reveal.** `"This is what $[amount] actually looks like."` Lead with the number the viewer feels.

**6. Trend callout.** `"Okay, so [thing] is doing [surprising trend]. Here's why."`

**7. Scene-set + pivot.** `"This is [visible current event]. But [bigger angle] is what people aren't talking about."`

### What makes an explainer hook different

A generic viral hook sells a reaction. An explainer hook must also plant a concrete anchor AND imply that a *system* exists to be revealed. Generic asks "watch what happens." Explainer asks "watch what happens, and find out why it works this way."

## Language

- **Short declarative sentences.** Fragments are fine for emphasis. "No nuclear stuff yet." "Check."
- **Present tense**, even for history. "This chokepoint *is* called," not "was called."
- **Second person** to implicate the viewer. "This is what $25 million *looks like*."
- **One clause, one idea.** No sentence carries two facts. Most viewing is muted, so the caption-reading brain must not lag the audio.
- **Numbers instead of adjectives.** "57 km," "100 million degrees," "20% of Americans." Concrete numbers process faster than description and need no inference.
- **No throat-clearing anywhere**, not just the hook. Every beat opens on the new information.

### Transition phrases that carry escalation

These are verbal, not written, which is why they work. They mimic someone excitedly explaining something to a friend.

- "But watch what happens."
- "So why don't we just [obvious solution]? Well, [complication]."
- "That is, until..."
- "The thing is..."
- "Okay, so..."
- "What's even crazier is..."

## Retention mechanics

- **Re-hook every 10-15 seconds.** Forward-pointing cues ("watch what happens," "How?") reset the open loop mid-script. One hook cannot carry 60 seconds.
- **Withhold the biggest fact until 75-90%.** Not earlier.
- **Visual change every 2-4 seconds.** A map zoom or a chart build counts; it does not need to be a hard cut. Frequent scene changes measure ~32% higher retention than static.
- **Captions and voiceover say the same thing at the same time.** Tested to beat either alone, because most vertical viewing is sound-off.

### Self-diagnosis

- Drops to ~10% by second 5 and flatlines: the hook failed, not the body.
- Flat after the hook: the pattern interrupts are working.

## Endings

**Default: land on the payoff and stop.** Summarizing at the end is a measured retention and rewatch killer.

Three patterns, in order of preference:

1. **Cold stop on the biggest line.** The payoff line IS the ending. No recap, no sign-off. This is the dominant pattern for evergreen explainers.
2. **Circular callback.** If the hook set up an explicit tension between two things, close by naming both sides in one sentence. This drives rewatch, because viewers replay to see how the setup foreshadowed the ending.
3. **Engagement question.** ONLY for topical or ongoing-story content. On an evergreen explainer it reads as padding.

Never put a call to action before the payoff. Deliver 100% of the substance first.

## Platform cuts

Build the master to the **45-60 second spec**, then derive:

| Platform | Cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Full 45-60s | The only one with real search discovery, so the title and on-screen text carry keyword weight. Best home for this format. |
| TikTok | 28-35s, aggressive | Drop the context beat, hook straight into escalation. 1-2 seconds to land the interrupt. |
| Reels | 28-35s | Completion drops sharply past 45s. Hook taps emotion or aesthetic more than a stated benefit. |
